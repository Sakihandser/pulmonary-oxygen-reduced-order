# Decision consistency and mechanistic ambiguity

Run `python certainty_extension/code/reproduce_certainty.py` from the numerical root. The complete runner is `python reproduce.py`. Nine CSVs, two figures and 13 tests accompany this extension.

A deterministic conclusion means that every model in a nonempty, explicitly constrained class yields the same answer to a declared question. An empty class is incompatible; two feasible models on opposite sides are a constructive ambiguity; numerical bounds that straddle a threshold without such witnesses are unresolved. A best-fitting point estimate is not a substitute for these distinctions.

The fixed-capacity class uses one shared distribution across arbitrary measurement flow ratios. Continuous cell outer programs and finite-node inner mixtures bound predictions. The default decision demonstrations assume exposure support [0.000001,32]. The minimum-capacity exclusion calculation instead adds an unbounded tail cell and includes zero exposure, so its lower bound does not impose that support cap. Dual witnesses include an amplified numerical guard; this is not formal interval arithmetic.

With baseline and r=1.25 and 1.5 residual halfwidths of 0.001, every admitted structure-A model exceeds the illustrative recovery target at r=2. Structure B excludes recovery even after one extra state. Structure A loses its guarantee at error 0.005 or with the stated additional Ce/consumption uncertainty. All conclusions are conditional on supplied assumptions, not patient outcomes. The full finite mixtures and grid-refinement history are retained.

A finite fixed-capacity mixture also approximates a homogeneous square-root capacity response over r in [0.5,2]. The classical Levy representation has infinite mean and is not a physical finite-capacity lung. Censoring and finite quantile quadrature give finite alternatives with a uniform residual-error bound. The 4097-path exposure-cap-32 construction has total effective capacity about 45.236 L/min versus homogeneous baseline capacity 8.047 L/min; thus an independently established capacity restriction may distinguish them. These effective capacities are not clinical DLCO or anatomical measurements.

For six synthetic response states and residual halfwidth 0.001, the minimum total fixed capacity is bracketed by roughly 18.1 and 18.6 L/min. An independently justified upper bound of 10 would therefore exclude the entire fixed-capacity class for these inputs, rather than only one fitted candidate. No such independent bound is available for the included patients.

Classical set-membership analysis, model invalidation and Laplace-mixture results are credited in the paper. The contribution is the explicit oxygen-modelling application, testable conditional decisions, and constructive countermodels; no claim of mathematical conceptual priority or external clinical validation is made.
