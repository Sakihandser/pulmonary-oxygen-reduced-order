# External oxygenation checks

The target is the direction and size of the arterial change conditional on the contemporaneous mixed-venous value. This is a retrospective targeted source search. Published results were visible during source selection. No external outcome chose a parameter, endpoint, tolerance, comparison law or observation to retain. Inclusion in the pressure test requires a source outside the calibration tables with repeated arterial and mixed-venous tensions, PaCO2 and an oxygen-independent flow ratio. Nishizaki and Farina are additional checks on oxygen saturation and perfusion partition. Stubbs had already been cited as contextual evidence and is explicitly labelled a secondary reuse. The studies and the measurement scales are evaluated separately.

Abdel Kafi et al. Tables 1 and 2, DOI 10.1016/S0735-1097(98)00091-6. All four published intervention contrasts are included, including the six-person subset with its own baseline. CI is in L/min/m² and pressures are in mmHg. The intervention CI ratio uses the reported means. Flow was measured by triplicate thermodilution and baseline catheterization was on room air. Use the existing gas mapping at FiO2 0.21, barometric pressure 760 and water vapour pressure 47 mmHg, assumed Hb 15 g/dL, Hill exponent 2.7, P50 26.8 mmHg and respiratory quotient 0.8. The 108 combinations use the same sensitivity grid as the primary analysis. For each combination we additionally shift the query-state P50 by −2, 0 or +2 mmHg while holding baseline P50 fixed. This tests a differential affinity change without claiming the shift was observed or separating arterial and venous pH effects. Freeze the baseline residual before supplying intervention flow, PvO2 and PaCO2. The target PaO2 is passed only to scoring. Score the frozen residual, the fixed-share envelope and two simple baselines (unchanged PaO2 and baseline PaO2 plus the change in PvO2). The same ±0.01 residual and ±10% ratio envelope tests external compatibility. A target outside that widened envelope challenges the model under the declared assumptions. Unweighted mean absolute errors summarize four correlated states and have no sampling interpretation. Tables report marginal group summaries, and shared institutions/authors across historical reports cannot establish individual independence.

Nishizaki et al. Table 2, DOI 10.1183/23120541.00725-2020. Use exercise-session thermodilution CI, catheter SvO2 and pulse oximetry. Do not use the earlier resting diagnostic Fick CI. Stubbs et al. Table 1, DOI 10.1183/23120541.00449-2021, enrolled nine IPAH and seven CTEPH patients. The flow method is not confirmed in this report. Both studies provide baseline and exercise means. For this simplified saturation-only check ignore dissolved oxygen, take a common capillary endpoint of 98, 99 or 100 percent, and calculate s0=(E−Sp0)/(E−Sv0). Feed exercise SvO2 and the ratio of mean flows to the forward equation. Predicted saturation relative to baseline is compared with the withheld exercise SpO2. These results cannot be interchanged with arterial blood-gas predictions or used as validated oximeter error limits.

Farina et al. Tables 4–6, DOI 10.3389/fcvm.2023.1241379. Four same-day stages in 18 group 1/4 PH participants at 0, 20, 40 and 60 percent of maximum workload. Impedance cardiac output and inert-gas rebreathing perfused-ventilated flow give a separate two-compartment proxy for shunt. Use the published shunt, including rounding, and retain the reported mean percentage as distinct from the ratio of means. A fixed baseline ratio of means gives a reference for absolute shunt flow. The proxy does not measure the model's total variation across all paths. Group 1 and group 4 have no separate results. Bratel et al. (DOI 10.1016/j.resp.2007.03.003) supply a separate qualitative inert-gas intervention check in the manuscript. Changes reported as nonsignificant were not assigned a zero effect.
