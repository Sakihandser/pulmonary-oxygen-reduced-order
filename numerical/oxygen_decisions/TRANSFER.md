# Blood curve and study transfer

Run `python oxygen_decisions/transfer_checks.py` from the extracted S2 archive. The root runner also checks the four generated tables against the archived values. These retrospective targeted comparisons keep all eligible stages in the named source tables. Published outcomes were visible at source selection. No follow-up arterial value selects a parameter.

The Brussels study supplies all four original contrasts and their original baseline flow ratios. Replace only the nominal Hill function with equation 1 of Severinghaus (1979), DOI 10.1152/jappl.1979.46.3.599. Retain 1.34 Hb for oxygen binding, 0.0031 times pressure for dissolved oxygen, and the original alveolar equation. Recompute each resting residual under the new function. This tests the consequences of changing the standard blood dissociation curve while holding exchange structure fixed. Neither curve corrects for actual within-stage temperature or pH. Differences of MAE use full precision. The rounding check varies only each observed follow-up pressure over ±0.5 mmHg, keeping all inputs fixed. The extrema of the difference of two absolute errors occur at an endpoint or a prediction inside the interval.

The pressure transfer records contain published means from seven patients with severe COPD and PH (Dantzker and D’Alonzo, 1986, PMID 3098145, abstract) and marginal medians from 36 invasive exercise tests for suspected pulmonary vascular disease (Alotaibi et al., 2024, Table 2). Neither sample is a replication in primary PAH. Use study-specific resting Pa, Pv, PaCO2 and follow-up Pv and PaCO2 in mmHg. Fix the resting fraction and predict the follow-up Pa without any cardiac output input. This permits both stages of the San Diego direct-Fick series without importing the test endpoint via flow. Use the same nominal room-air, pressure, Hb and R assumptions as Brussels. These are scenario assumptions, not measured values for every participant. The Hill grid crosses all 108 baseline settings with all three follow-up affinity shifts. A nonphysical resting residual or reversed endpoint gradient is marked infeasible, with the reasons defined by the tests in predict(). The standard Severinghaus comparison uses nominal conditions. No follow-up target enters calibration, the alveolar estimate or these feasibility tests. The table of scores includes unchanged-Pa and venous-change errors for each contrast. Do not combine means and marginal medians or interpret plug-in predictions as individual responses.

The historical arterial saturation check uses Table 1 (case mix and repeat visit) and Table 3, pp. 24–25, in Gazetopoulos et al. (1974), DOI 10.1136/hrt.36.1.19. Cases 1–9 comprise the source-defined primary/thromboembolic stratum. Cases 10–12 were postoperative and 13–23 had obstructive lung disease. For the main direction count take the first visit and lowest reported exercise load per person with a paired arterial saturation, and use the published decimal precision to identify ties. Case 9 lacks an exercise endpoint. Retain all other measured workloads and both visits of case 2 in the source CSV and changes table. A tie is an equal printed value. The row-level differences describe arterial saturation, independent of modelling, flow or inference of venous oxygen. They do not score a pressure prediction. The historical source did not present paired mixed-venous pressure for this test.
